Before you run

Check in. Don't just show up.

Before the start, don't only ask: “What's my target pace?”

    02

    During the race

    Not every bad feeling means stop. But not every bad feeling should be ignored.

    Green

    “I feel challenged, but in control.”

    • Breathing is up but controlled
    • Muscles are tired but movement is normal
    • Effort feels hard but matches the plan
    • Still thinking and responding well
    Keep moving.

    Yellow

    “Something has changed.”

    • Pace feels much harder
    • Coordination or movement starts to change
    • Dizziness or nausea
    • Cramps that get in the way
    • Feeling very weak
    • Body heat feels unusual
    • Pain changes the way you run
    Slow down → Check → Decide.
    03

    The 30-second reset

    When a yellow signal shows up, do this:

    1

    Stop

    Ease the pace or stop.

    2

    Breathe

    Take a few breaths and don't force yourself straight back in.

    3

    Check

    Ask yourself:

    BodyWhat has changed?
    BrainCan I still think and respond normally?
    MovementIs my movement normal, or am I compensating?
    TrendBetter, the same, or getting worse?
    4

    Decide

    Continue / Slow down / Stop & seek help

    04

    Red signal

    Some signals are not a test of mental toughness.

    This isn't the time to prove yourself. It's the time to listen to your body.

    Stop + get help

    If you notice:

    • Chest pain or pressure
    • Heart rate that won't come down at rest
    • Unusual or severe breathlessness
    • Fainting / near-fainting
    • Confusion or a change in consciousness
    • Clear loss of coordination
    • Getting worse even after slowing down or stopping
    • Signs of serious heat illness (confusion, loss of consciousness)
    Don't push through. Get help.

    Know your event's medical points. Save the emergency contact / medical post number before the race.

      1-Minute Runner Check

      Come → choose → get your insight

      Before your next run, which one do you ignore most?

      You tend to ignore fatigue.

      Feeling tired isn't weakness. It's information. Your running problem may not be your training plan. It may be your decision-making.

      Try this: before you start, rate your energy from 1 to 10. If it's low, make today an easy day, not a test.

      You tend to push through pain.

      Discomfort from effort and pain that changes how you move are not the same thing. The second one deserves a pause.

      Try this: ask “is this getting worse as I go?” If it is, slow down and assess. If pain is sharp, doesn't go away or keeps coming back, stop and talk to a health professional.

      You tend to skip sleep.

      Recovery happens when you rest, not when you run. A short night changes how hard the same pace feels.

      Try this: after a poor night, keep the run but lower the intensity. Rest day or lazy day? Sometimes rest is the training.

      You tend to forget hydration.

      Thirst often shows up late, and on the trail your next drink may be further away than you think.

      Try this: plan your drinks before the run, not during. Check where the water points are on your route.

      This is general guidance to help you reflect, not medical advice.

      The Runner Decision Test

      Your body says slow down. Your friends say GAS!

      A 2 minute scenario. There's no trick answer. Pick what you'd honestly do.

      KM 8. Your pace is still good.
      You have to work tomorrow.
      Your friend shouts: “Gas! Only 2 km left!”
      Your knee is starting to feel uncomfortable.
      What would you do?

      You chose to push.

      Honest answer, and a very common one. Now ask: who made that decision? Your knee, your goal, or the voice that doesn't want to look weak in front of friends?

      Feel → Think → Decide: notice the knee, think about what's driving you, then decide. Pushing can be fine when it's a choice, not a reflex.

      You chose to slow down and assess.

      You gave yourself time to collect information before committing. That's self-regulation in action.

      Next step: while you slow down, ask “is this getting better or worse?” Let the answer, not the crowd, decide the last 2 km.

      You chose to stop and reassess.

      Stopping takes courage when everyone around you is going. Remember tomorrow's work, and next month's runs.

      Remember: stopping to check isn't quitting. It's how you make sure you keep running for years.

      You said “it depends”.

      Exactly. On what, though? The kind of pain, how it changes, what tomorrow needs from you, what this race means to you.

      That's Feel → Think → Decide. “It depends” is the start of a good decision, as long as you actually go and check.

      We're not asking who's “most correct”. We're asking how you make a better decision when your body, your running goals and social pressure all meet.
